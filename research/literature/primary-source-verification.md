### C01: Safe Policy Improvement with Baseline Bootstrapping (SPIBB)

1. **Bibliographic record:**
   - **Title:** Safe Policy Improvement with Baseline Bootstrapping
   - **Authors:** Romain Laroche, Paul Trichelair, Rémi Tachet des Combes (Microsoft Research Montreal)
   - **Publication year & venue:** 2019; *Proceedings of the 36th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2019)*, Long Beach, California. PMLR 97:3652–3661.
   - **Primary locator:** [https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/laroche19a.html](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/laroche19a.html); arXiv:1905.12110 [cs.LG].
   - **Status:** **VERIFIED PRIMARY RECORD**.

2. **Formal problem and mathematical assumptions:**
   - **Problem setting:** Batch (offline) Reinforcement Learning in a Markov Decision Process $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, R, \gamma \rangle$. A batch dataset $\mathcal{D}$ has been generated by an existing baseline policy $\pi_b$.
   - **Objective:** Learn a target policy $\pi$ that maximizes expected return $\rho(\pi)$ while guaranteeing **Safe Policy Improvement (SPI)** with high probability:
     $$\mathbb{P}(\rho(\pi) \ge \rho(\pi_b) - \zeta) \ge 1 - \delta$$
     where $\zeta \ge 0$ is a small allowable degradation margin and $\delta \in (0, 1)$ is the confidence parameter.
   - **Assumptions:** Known or consistently estimable baseline policy $\pi_b$; discrete state-action transitions; finite batch dataset $\mathcal{D}$.
   - **Key mechanism:** The SPIBB algorithm partitions state-action space into "safe" (frequently observed in $\mathcal{D}$, count $N(s, a) \ge N_\wedge$) and "uncertain" regions ($N(s, a) < N_\wedge$). For uncertain pairs, the learned policy is strictly constrained to copy (bootstrap from) the baseline policy: $\pi(a \mid s) = \pi_b(a \mid s)$. In safe regions, $\pi$ is optimized via dynamic programming on the empirical MDP.
   - **Experimental evidence:** Validated on synthetic gridworlds and discrete Atari benchmarks using SPIBB-DQN.

3. **Comparison with CausaGuard:**
   - **Overlaps:** CausaGuard's conservative wrappers (v0.2–v0.52) and fallback to deterministic repair/escalation mirror the core SPIBB philosophy: when predictive models are uncertain (or risk exceeds $R_{\text{limit}}$), default to a trusted baseline rather than taking an uncalibrated intervention.
   - **Differences:** SPIBB operates in sequential offline MDPs where uncertainty is driven by state-action visit counts $N(s, a)$. CausaGuard evaluates a 2-step decision under *active observation acquisition* (paying for verification), where uncertainty stems from *sensor noise, delay, and hidden state*, not merely unvisited batch states.
   - **Contribution classification:**
     - Baseline fallback principle: **ALREADY ESTABLISHED** (Laroche et al., 2019).
     - Application to fallible runtime verification for agents: **PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED** (conceptually sound, but requires empirical validation).
   - **Required published baseline:** $\Pi_{\le b}\text{-SPIBB}$ must be recognized as the theoretical gold standard for offline safe baseline comparison. In CausaGuard Stage 1/2, learned selectors must explicitly prove SPI over deterministic heuristics.

### C02: Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning

1. **Bibliographic record:**
   - **Title:** Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning
   - **Authors:** Miroslav Dudík, John Langford, Lihong Li (Yahoo! Research)
   - **Publication year & venue:** 2011; *Proceedings of the 28th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2011)*, Bellevue, Washington. Extended journal version in *Statistical Science* 29(4):519–538, 2014.
   - **Primary locator:** [https://icml.cc/2011/papers/554_icmlpaper.pdf](https://icml.cc/2011/papers/554_icmlpaper.pdf); arXiv:1103.4601 [cs.LG].
   - **Status:** **VERIFIED PRIMARY RECORD**.

2. **Formal problem and mathematical assumptions:**
   - **Problem setting:** Contextual bandits. Context $x \sim \mathcal{D}$, action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, stochastic reward $r \in [0, 1]$. Historical logged dataset collected under logging policy $h_0(a \mid x)$ with propensities $p_a = h_0(a \mid x)$.
   - **Objective:** Evaluate the expected value $V(h) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}, a \sim h(x)}[r(x, a)]$ of a target policy $h$ without online deployment.
   - **Estimator formulation (Doubly Robust):**
     $$\hat{V}_{\text{DR}}(h) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \left( \hat{r}(x_i, h(x_i)) + \frac{\mathbb{I}(a_i = h(x_i))}{p_i} (r_i - \hat{r}(x_i, a_i)) \right)$$
     where $\hat{r}(x, a)$ is a reward prediction model (Direct Method) and $p_i$ is the propensity score.
   - **Assumptions:** Strict common support / overlap ($h(a \mid x) > 0 \implies h_0(a \mid x) > 0$ for all $x$); unconfoundedness (no unobserved confounders influencing both action choice and reward).
   - **Properties:** Unbiased if *either* the reward model $\hat{r}$ is correct *or* logging propensities $p_a$ are correct. Achieves significantly lower variance than pure Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS).

3. **Comparison with CausaGuard:**
   - **Overlaps:** CausaGuard roadmap Objective O5 and research plan emphasize off-policy evaluation from logged agent interventions.
   - **Differences:** CausaGuard v0.5–v0.52 generated *all-arm potential outcomes* in simulation by evaluating all macro actions on the same seed tape. As noted in [evidence-map.md](../foundations/evidence-map.md), this is *not* off-policy causal inference; it is paired counterfactual simulation. Dudík et al. address the genuine factual-only observational log problem where only $r(x_i, a_i)$ is observed.
   - **Contribution classification:**
     - Off-policy evaluation via Doubly Robust estimation: **ALREADY ESTABLISHED** (Dudík et al., 2011).
     - Claiming causal effect identification from CausaGuard's current all-arm simulator: **UNSUPPORTED** (the simulator bypasses the fundamental identification problem of causal inference).
   - **Required published baseline:** Any future study evaluating logged observational agent traces must implement $\hat{V}_{\text{DR}}$ with explicit propensity logging and overlap checking.

### C03: Conformal Risk Control (CRC)

1. **Bibliographic record:**
   - **Title:** Conformal Risk Control
   - **Authors:** Anastasios N. Angelopoulos, Stephen Bates, Adam Fisch, Lihua Lei, Tal Schuster (UC Berkeley, MIT)
   - **Publication year & venue:** 2024; *Twelfth International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024)*, Spotlight.
   - **Primary locator:** [https://openreview.net/forum?id=g090V3xPdD](https://openreview.net/forum?id=g090V3xPdD); arXiv:2208.02814 [cs.LG].
   - **Status:** **VERIFIED PRIMARY RECORD**.

2. **Formal problem and mathematical assumptions:**
   - **Problem setting:** Supervised learning and structured prediction. Input $X$, output $Y$, and a candidate prediction set or multi-threshold decision $C_\lambda(X)$ indexed by parameter $\lambda \in \Lambda$.
   - **Loss function:** General loss $\ell(C_\lambda(X), Y) \in [0, B]$ that is non-increasing as $\lambda$ allows more conservative predictions (e.g., false negative rate, token error, safety violation).
   - **Objective:** Select $\hat{\lambda}$ on calibration data such that the expected test risk is bounded by a user-specified limit $\alpha$:
     $$\mathbb{E}[\ell(C_{\hat{\lambda}}(X_{n+1}), Y_{n+1})] \le \alpha$$
   - **Assumptions:** Exchangeability of calibration and test samples $(X_i, Y_i)_{i=1}^{n+1}$; monotonic loss in $\lambda$.
   - **Algorithm:** Compute empirical risk $\hat{R}_n(\lambda) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell_i(\lambda)$ and choose $\hat{\lambda} = \inf \{ \lambda : \frac{n}{n+1}\hat{R}_n(\lambda) + \frac{B}{n+1} \le \alpha \}$. Provides exact, distribution-free, finite-sample guarantees.

3. **Comparison with CausaGuard:**
   - **Overlaps:** CausaGuard's risk-gated policies (`f16_cal_r05`, `f16_raw_r05`) threshold predicted probability of harm at $R_{\text{limit}} = 0.05$. In v0.5–v0.52, CausaGuard used isotonic calibration to estimate risk probabilities, then checked whether observed test risk satisfied $\le 0.05$.
   - **Differences:** CRC provides a *provable distribution-free guarantee* on expected risk $\mathbb{E}[\text{loss}] \le \alpha$ on exchangeable data. CausaGuard's isotonic thresholding provides *no finite-sample risk coverage guarantee*, which is precisely why v0.51 and v0.52 exhibited empirical risk threshold slippage under distribution shift.
   - **Contribution classification:**
     - Provable risk thresholding via calibration sets: **ALREADY ESTABLISHED** (Angelopoulos et al., 2024).
     - Ad-hoc isotonic thresholding without conformal guarantees: **OBSOLETE / WEAK**.
   - **Required published baseline:** Conformal Risk Control should replace or serve as the rigorous benchmark against empirical isotonic regression for setting the intervention threshold $\hat{\lambda}$ in Stage 2.

### C04: AgentDojo: Benchmark for LLM Agent Security

1. **Bibliographic record:**
   - **Title:** AgentDojo: A Dynamic Environment to Evaluate Prompt Injection Attacks and Defenses for LLM Agents
   - **Authors:** Edoardo Debenedetti, Jie Zhang, Mislav Balunovic, Luca Beurer-Kellner, Marc Fischer, Florian Tramèr (ETH Zurich, Invariant Labs)
   - **Publication year & venue:** 2024; *Thirty-eighth Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2024) Datasets and Benchmarks Track*.
   - **Primary locator:** [https://openreview.net/forum?id=7U697qBuhz](https://openreview.net/forum?id=7U697qBuhz); arXiv:2406.13314 [cs.CR].
   - **Status:** **VERIFIED PRIMARY RECORD**.

2. **Formal problem and benchmark design:**
   - **Problem setting:** Dynamic security evaluation of LLM agents equipped with tools facing indirect prompt injection attacks.
   - **Benchmark characteristics:** 97 realistic tasks across 4 application suites (email client, e-banking, travel booking, workspace management) with 629 security test cases and 19 defense techniques.
   - **Evaluation methodology:** Measures both **Utility** (benign task completion percentage) and **Security** (defense against unauthorized adversarial tool execution or data exfiltration).
   - **Key findings:** Current LLM agents exhibit high vulnerability; defenses often reduce utility significantly (the utility–security tradeoff); state-of-the-art defenses fail to defend against multi-stage adaptive attacks.

3. **Comparison with CausaGuard:**
   - **Overlaps:** Evaluates agent tool calls, distinguishes utility from security violations, and models attacks embedded in data.
   - **Differences:** AgentDojo evaluates prompt injection attacks on full LLMs (GPT-4, Claude) interacting with complex mock APIs. CausaGuard evaluates a lower-level decision problem: an abstract tabular/contextual intervention selector operating on operational covariates (staleness, load, alarm, cost) under costly fallible verification. AgentDojo has no economic latency/verification cost model, no formal value-of-information optimization, and no all-arm counterfactual potential outcome simulator.
   - **Contribution classification:**
     - Dynamic LLM agent security benchmark: **ALREADY ESTABLISHED** (Debenedetti et al., 2024).
     - Modeling verification costs, latency deadlines, and economic recovery trade-offs: **POTENTIALLY NOVEL COMPLEMENT** if connected to agent workflows.
   - **Required published baseline:** AgentDojo's task completion and attack success metrics represent the community standard for empirical agent security.

### C05: Defeating Prompt Injections by Design (CaMeL)

1. **Bibliographic record:**
   - **Title:** Defeating Prompt Injections by Design
   - **Authors:** Edoardo Debenedetti, Ilia Shumailov, Tianqi Fan, Jamie Hayes, Nicholas Carlini, Daniel Fabian, Christoph Kern, Chongyang Shi, Andreas Terzis, Florian Tramèr (Google, Google DeepMind, ETH Zurich)
   - **Publication year & venue:** March 2025; arXiv preprint.
   - **Primary locator:** [https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813); Code at `github.com/google-research/camel-prompt-injection`.
   - **Status:** **VERIFIED PRIMARY RECORD**.

2. **Formal problem and architecture:**
   - **Problem setting:** Elimination of prompt injection vulnerabilities in LLM agents by structural system design rather than heuristic prompt filtering.
   - **Architecture:** CaMeL (Capabilities for Machine Learning) enforces strict **information flow control (IFC)** and capability-based security:
     1. Strict separation of *control flow* (derived only from trusted user instructions) and *data flow* (untrusted inputs retrieved from tools).
     2. Untrusted data can never become executable instructions or alter the sequence of tool dispatch.
     3. Two-tier LLM execution: a privileged planner/interpreter and a quarantined worker.
     4. Cryptographic/runtime capability tokens required before executing sensitive tools.
   - **Guarantees:** Provable non-interference: indirect prompt injections embedded in data cannot force execution of unauthorized actions or exfiltrate private data.

3. **Comparison with CausaGuard:**
   - **Overlaps:** CausaGuard's immutable permission layer (`MockState`, `access()`, and `grade_access()` in `intervention_env.py`) mirrors CaMeL's principle: authorization is a trusted, non-bypassable boundary that model predictions or data payloads can never override.
   - **Differences:** CaMeL is a comprehensive architectural security framework for LLM execution flow. CausaGuard's permission check is a simple in-memory mock schema/ACL checker designed to verify that policy selectors cannot bypass authorization. CaMeL does not address cost-optimal verification, Bayesian evidence updating, or SLA latency deadlines.
   - **Contribution classification:**
     - Non-bypassable structural capability enforcement: **ALREADY ESTABLISHED** (Debenedetti et al., 2025).
     - Claiming that CausaGuard invented runtime authorization dominance: **UNSUPPORTED**.
   - **Required published baseline:** CausaGuard must explicitly cite CaMeL as the conceptual foundation for runtime capability enforcement and frame its own access grader as a domain-level instantiation of capability boundaries.

### C06: ToolEmu: Identifying the Risks of LM Agents with an LM-Emulated Sandbox

1. **Bibliographic record:**
   - **Title:** ToolEmu: Identifying the Risks of LM Agents with an LM-Emulated Sandbox
   - **Authors:** Yangjun Ruan, Honghua Dong, Andrew Wang, Silviu Pitis, Yongchao Zhou, Jimmy Ba, Yann Dubois, Chris J. Maddison, Tatsunori Hashimoto (University of Toronto, Vector Institute, Stanford University)
   - **Publication year & venue:** 2024; *Twelfth International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024)*, Spotlight.
   - **Primary locator:** [https://openreview.net/forum?id=jTafc1Ag6w](https://openreview.net/forum?id=jTafc1Ag6w); arXiv:2309.15817 [cs.AI].
   - **Status:** **VERIFIED PRIMARY RECORD**.

2. **Formal problem and methodology:**
   - **Problem setting:** Scalable risk assessment of tool-using language model agents in high-stakes environments without implementing thousands of physical sandboxes.
   - **Methodology:** Employs an LLM (e.g. GPT-4) as an interactive virtual sandbox emulator:
     - Generates realistic, stateful tool execution responses based on tool definitions and agent actions.
     - Automatically flags safety violations, data corruption, and unintended side effects via an independent LLM safety evaluator.
   - **Benchmark:** 36 high-stakes toolkits (financial, smart home, healthcare, system admin) and 144 diverse risk scenarios.

3. **Comparison with CausaGuard:**
   - **Overlaps:** Uses simulated environments to evaluate agent action safety, with an independent grader evaluating safety violations separately from agent execution.
   - **Differences:** ToolEmu relies on LLM-prompted world emulation, which is subjective, non-deterministic, and prone to emulator hallucination. CausaGuard uses an exact, deterministic mathematical/algorithmic simulator with explicit parametric failure models, latency curves, and strict cryptographic replay guarantees.
   - **Contribution classification:**
     - Agent risk evaluation via simulated sandboxes: **ALREADY ESTABLISHED** (Ruan et al., 2024).
     - Exact, reproducible, counterfactual potential-outcome simulation with explicit latency/economic cost tradeoffs: **POTENTIALLY NOVEL NICHE** for decision-theoretic benchmarking.
   - **Required published baseline:** ToolEmu represents the primary comparator for agent safety evaluation sandboxes.

## 3. Conceptual synthesis of search queries Q1–Q4

### Q1 & Q2: Value of Information (VOI) and POMDP sequential inspection
- **Foundational literature:** Howard (1966), Raiffa & Schlaifer (1961), Blackwell (1953), Cassandra et al. (1994), Kaelbling et al. (1998).
- **Core finding:** Formulating sequential decision-making where an agent decides whether to pay cost $c$ for a noisy/fallible inspection signal $E$ to reduce state uncertainty before selecting a high-stakes action is **the classical, textbook Value of Information (VOI) problem in POMDPs**.
- **Crucial implication for CausaGuard:** CausaGuard **did not invent** the sequential verification-then-decision formulation. Claiming that selecting actions based on fallible verification under cost and delay is an original algorithmic paradigm is **COMPLETELY UNSUPPORTED**. This decision model has been studied for 60 years in operations research, active sensing, and structural health monitoring.
- **Valid scientific framing:** CausaGuard is an **applied benchmark and empirical study** of classical VOI principles applied to modern AI agent workflows facing verifier degradation, latency deadlines, and capability-based security boundaries.

### Q3: Selective prediction, learning to defer, and active feature acquisition
- **Foundational literature:** Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017), Cortes et al. (2016), Mozannar & Sontag (2020), Charoenphakdee et al. (2021).
- **Core finding:** Multi-stage learning to defer and Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) explicitly model paying a financial or latency cost to acquire missing features (or human verification) before committing to a prediction.
- **Crucial implication for CausaGuard:** When CausaGuard evaluates `escalate` (defer to human specialist) or `verify` (acquire additional feature), it is instantiating a cost-sensitive multi-option learning-to-defer problem. The baseline suite must acknowledge this connection.

### Q4: Safe policy improvement and off-policy evaluation
- **Foundational literature:** Dudík et al. (2011), Thomas et al. (2015), Laroche et al. (2019), Angelopoulos et al. (2024).
- **Core finding:** Safe policy improvement requires provable performance bounds relative to an existing baseline. CausaGuard's repeated empirical finding that learned models fail to beat simple deterministic heuristics (`rules_repair`, `cost_rule`) is consistent with the offline RL literature: under limited support or distribution shift, naive regression and uncalibrated models degrade performance.

## 4. Key conclusions for CausaGuard research foundations

1. **The core decision formulation is classical:** Sequential verification under cost and delay is POMDP Value of Information. CausaGuard must present its Stage 1 baseline (`cost_rule`) as an analytical instantiation of classical Bayesian VOI, not as a proprietary novel invention.
2. **The "Intervention Paradox" is a recognized frontier:** Recent literature (*Accurate Failure Prediction in Agents Does Not Imply Effective Failure Prevention*, arXiv:2602.03338) proves that predicting failures does not guarantee effective intervention. CausaGuard's negative results in v0.51 and v0.52 (where ML risk models failed to produce net utility improvements) directly reflect this paradox.
3. **The genuine contribution lies in the benchmark and the capability boundary:** What is genuinely missing in current literature is an **independently graded, counterfactually paired benchmark** that tests whether VOI and safe policy improvement can manage the disruption–recovery tradeoff in agentic customer support and data engineering workflows under distribution shift and strict capability enforcement.
